Separately, the sharp sell-off in Kioxia and other memory-chip names — triggered by TSMC's expanded capex plans — suggests markets are starting to ask whether chip supply is catching up with, or even overtaking, AI demand, a shift from the pure "AI-boom" narrative of recent months. Medium term, the key questions are whether elevated energy costs complicate central bank decisions — including the Bank of Japan's July 30–31 meeting — and whether AI-related capital spending translates into durable earnings, or proves to have outrun near-term demand in some corners of the chip supply chain.
