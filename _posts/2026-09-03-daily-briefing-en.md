## Compass Insight

The through-line is mechanical: a war narrows oil supply, higher energy prices lift inflation, and central banks answer with higher rates. That chain now reaches household budgets far from the Gulf — through fuel and imported goods on one side, and through mortgage, auto-loan and government borrowing costs on the other. The near-term test is a cluster of September central-bank meetings (ECB, Bank of Japan, Fed): the question is how far each is willing to lean toward hikes. The longer-term hinge is the Strait of Hormuz. If traffic normalizes, the upward pressure on yields should fade; if the standoff drags on, markets keep pricing a world of stickier prices and higher-for-longer rates. Watch two words: energy and rates.
