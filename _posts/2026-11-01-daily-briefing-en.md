## Top U.S. Stories

- **Tech rout.** The Nasdaq Composite [fell 2.9% on Friday](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/amazon-intel-earnings-big-tech-ai-spending-concerns.html) as investors worried that heavy AI spending may not pay off. The S&P 500 lost 1.8% and the Dow 1.1%.
  *Why it matters:* Big tech has carried the market for years. If investors start demanding proof of returns on AI spending, the leadership of the whole rally is in question.
- **Intel's worst day in decades.** Shares fell about 29% after the chipmaker guided to [$15–16 billion in revenue and 25 cents adjusted EPS](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/amazon-intel-earnings-big-tech-ai-spending-concerns.html), versus expectations of $17.1 billion and 43 cents. It cited a plant shutdown in Israel and soft PC demand.
- **Amazon misses.** Third-quarter revenue and earnings fell short, free cash flow was negative for a second straight quarter, and the company kept its [$220 billion 2026 capex forecast](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/amazon-intel-earnings-big-tech-ai-spending-concerns.html). Alphabet had [slid about 12% on Thursday](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nasdaq-futures-tumble-tech-earnings-ai/).
  *Why it matters:* Back-to-back disappointments from the biggest AI spenders sharpen the debate over whether the investment boom is getting ahead of its payoff.
- **Fed holds, hike risk grows.** The Fed [kept rates at 3.75%–4.00%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/fed-interest-rate-decision-october-2026.html) on Oct. 28 in a 10–2 vote, with two officials preferring a quarter-point hike. Many economists now [expect a hike as soon as December](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nasdaq-futures-tumble-tech-earnings-ai/).
  *Why it matters:* Higher-for-longer rates are a headwind for richly valued growth stocks and for borrowers.
- **Midterms Tuesday.** U.S. midterm elections are on Nov. 3, and energy traders are [positioning ahead of them](https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/news/world-policy/article/2026/10/30/wti-15-october-fears-iran-war).

## Compass Insight

Friday's selloff was less about one bad quarter than about a changing question: not "who is spending on AI?" but "who is getting paid for it?" Intel's weak outlook and Amazon's cash burn landed one day after Alphabet's slump, turning isolated disappointments into a theme. What makes this more delicate is the backdrop: oil is near $104 on Gulf shipping attacks, and the Fed leans toward raising rates, not cutting. In the short run, watch how Monday's Asian and European trading digests the Nasdaq drop and what the midterms mean for policy. Over the medium term, the test is whether AI leaders can show revenue growth that justifies their capital spending. Elevated oil plus rising rate expectations is a combination that can squeeze both household budgets and valuations.
