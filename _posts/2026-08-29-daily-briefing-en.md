## Top U.S. Stories

- **Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, in his first Jackson Hole speech, said inflation is "still too high"** and warned that if the central bank cannot be confident underlying inflation is falling toward its 2% target "clearly and at sufficient speed," then "we have work to do." He recommitted to the 2% PCE target and came about as close as he is likely to get to signaling a rate hike, [while stopping short of explicit forward guidance](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/28/kevin-warsh-jackson-hole-federal-reserve-inflation.html).
  *Why it matters:* Markets had been leaning toward the Fed being done tightening. A chair openly weighing hikes reintroduces two-way risk to bonds, mortgages and the dollar.
- **President Trump signed an executive order directing the government to rename Lake Ontario "Lake America,"** [escalating a trade war with Canada](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/27/g-s1-140583/trump-lake-ontario-america) that now includes 50% U.S. tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods. Canadian PM Mark Carney said his country will "forever" call it Lake Ontario.
  *Why it matters:* The tariff escalation, more than the name, raises costs for cross-border manufacturing and adds to the inflation picture the Fed is worried about.
- **At least 90 Americans are among more than 500 foreign nationals missing** after glacier-triggered flash floods on the Nepal–Tibet border; [the toll has climbed past 580 dead with roughly 1,900 unaccounted for in Nepal](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-28/nepal-tibet-floods-live-updates/107086974).
- **Wall Street slipped Friday but held onto weekly gains.** The S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,711.76 and the Nasdaq lost 0.52% to 26,402.42, dragged by chip stocks; the Dow was essentially flat at 53,559.99.
- **Nvidia** remained the market's center of gravity after Wednesday's earnings, in which it guided to about 70% revenue growth for fiscal 2028. Shares jumped 8.7% Thursday, then gave some back Friday.

## Compass Insight

The through-line this week is a policy picture getting *more* uncertain, not less. A Fed chair openly discussing hikes, a Bank of Japan signaling a possible September move, and a widening U.S.–Canada tariff fight all point the same way: upward pressure on prices and interest rates. For U.S. households, that means mortgage rates and Treasury yields may not fall as fast as hoped, and tariff-driven goods inflation is a live risk into the autumn. Near term, watch Warsh's follow-through and the September FOMC and BOJ meetings for whether "higher for longer" hardens into "higher again." Longer term, the reappearance of real yields on cash and bonds is a reason for savers to revisit how much they hold in each — not a call to trade around headlines.
