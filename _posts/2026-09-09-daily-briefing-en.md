## Compass Insight

The through-line today is simple: Middle East supply risk is back in the driver's seat. A Houthi strike on Saudi facilities that handle hundreds of thousands of barrels a day was enough to push Brent toward $98 and knock roughly 1% off the major U.S. indexes. The near-term question is mechanical — how fast Riyadh restores operations, and whether the U.S.–Iran exchange stays contained. If barrels return within days, the spike can fade; if not, the market will keep pricing a risk premium. The bigger issue is the collision of a crude rally with Thursday's CPI print. A hotter-than-expected number would let markets question a September rate cut, and that repricing would ripple through stocks, bonds and the dollar at once. For now, watch three things: Saudi repair timelines, the CPI release, and whether crude holds above the low-$90s for WTI. None of this is a call to act — it is a map of where the pressure is building.
