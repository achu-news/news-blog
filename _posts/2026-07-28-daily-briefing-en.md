## Top U.S. Stories

- The United States and Iran paused military strikes for a second straight day, following nearly two weeks of intense fighting. President Trump said the pause came "at the regime's request," while Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesperson said Tehran is "currently not negotiating" with Washington, insisting official talks remain limited to Oman-mediated discussions over the Strait of Hormuz ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/27/us-iran-war-trump-hormuz.html)). *Why it matters:* the fragile pause is the single biggest driver behind today's market rally — and could reverse just as quickly if talks collapse.
- A mass shooting at Seattle's "Bite of Seattle" food festival near the Space Needle left three people dead — a 19-year-old man, a 44-year-old man, and a 56-year-old woman — and four wounded, including a 2-year-old boy. A 15-year-old suspect was detained; a second suspect remains at large ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/seattle-shooting-bite-of-seattle/), [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/07/27/seattle-shooting-food-festival-suspects-victims/)).
- The FOMC begins its two-day meeting on July 28 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm)), with markets broadly expecting the Fed to hold rates steady after softer June inflation data gave policymakers room to delay tightening.
- Nvidia shares dropped roughly 5% on Monday, dragging the Nasdaq Composite lower even as the Dow and S&P 500 posted gains; SanDisk plunged 11% on memory-demand weakness ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/07/27/stock-market-today-july-27-dow-rises-on-oil-retreat-and-sandisk-plunges-11-on-memory-weakness/)).

## World & Europe

- A van rammed into crowds at a Berlin Pride event on July 26, killing one person and injuring 29. The suspect, 21-year-old Abdul Ballout, was later shot dead by police after a confrontation in Spandau. Germany's interior minister called it a likely "Islamist terror attack" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/26/berlin-police-shoot-dead-suspect-in-car-ramming-attack-on-pride-event), [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/07/26/berlin-pride-attack-investigated-terrorism-with-suspect-large/)). *Why it matters:* it's the second major security incident to rattle a European capital in recent days, alongside wildfire evacuations of roughly 50,000 people in southwest France ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/93678a31ff53fc46564b6dfd9934eae1)).
- In Japan, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said at a press conference on the evening of July 27 that her government would "swiftly submit legislation" to cut the consumption tax on food to 1% starting April 2027, for a two-year period ([NHK](https://news.web.nhk/newsweb/na/na-k10015187261000)). *Why it matters:* it's a direct attempt to ease household costs at a time when a weak yen is pushing up import and energy prices.

## Compass Insight

The story behind today's market move is how quickly geopolitical risk can unwind — and how easily it could snap back. A single de-escalation headline knocked over 5% off crude and lifted equities worldwide, yet Iran's own denial that formal talks are underway is a reminder that this calm rests on a fragile, reversible truce rather than a settled outcome.

For households, cheaper oil offers near-term relief at the pump and on utility bills, but a yen still parked near 163 to the dollar keeps import and energy costs elevated — the exact pressure Japan's proposed food consumption-tax cut is meant to offset, assuming it clears the legislative process. Short term, expect headlines out of the US-Iran talks and Wednesday's FOMC decision to keep driving volatility. Medium term, the durability of the ceasefire, the yen's trajectory, and the details of Japan's tax relief plan are the threads worth watching.
